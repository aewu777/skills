# Researching a character

Read this when no card exists for the character yet. The goal is to learn who they really are well enough to fill every heading of `card-template.md`, so the portrayal is faithful rather than a generic stand-in. A guessed character comes out as a stereotype, and users notice immediately.

## 1. Pin down which character

If the name is ambiguous, meaning it could be several characters or works (a common name, a remake, a character who appears in more than one series) and the user gave no source, ask which one in a single short question. Step out of role for this: you are not in character yet, and a wrong guess wastes the whole conversation. Once the source is clear, continue.

## 2. Decide whether your own knowledge is enough

Check yourself on two things: personality (temperament, values, flaws, how they treat people) and speech (how they actually talk).

- Confident on both: write the card from what you know. Searching adds little and slows the user down. Put `model knowledge` under sources.
- Vague, partial, or you only know the character by name: search before writing anything. A half-remembered character is where invented traits creep in.
- Never heard of them: you must search; see step 5 if you cannot.

Be honest in this check. Recognizing a name is not the same as knowing the person.

## 3. What to look for

If you can search the web, prefer the fandom or wiki page for the character, since it is written by people who know the work. Read:

- the personality and relationships sections, for temperament and for how they treat strangers, friends, and rivals;
- the background section only as far as it explains behavior;
- a quotes page, for speech *style only*: sentence length, vocabulary, tics, how they address people. Do not harvest lines to reuse.

Two or three good pages beat ten skimmed ones. Stop when you can fill every heading with something specific.

## 4. Always pin age and minor status

Find the character's age explicitly, even when the user never mentions it, and record it in the `Identity` section as the age, or `unclear` if the source does not say. Set `is_minor` to `yes`, `no`, or `unclear`. This is needed because the boundary around romance or intimacy depends on it: a minor or a character of unclear age gets no romantic or sexual scenes, and the card is the only place that fact survives across sessions. Ages that are stated, hinted by school year, or contradicted between sources should be noted as such. Do not default to "adult" because the character looks or acts mature.

## 5. If you cannot search and are not confident

Step out of role and say so plainly: you do not know this character well enough and cannot look them up. Ask the user for a link or a short description (personality, how they talk, who they are). Do not invent a personality to keep the scene going. An invented character misrepresents the source and the user may not realize it is made up. Once they supply material, build the card from it.

## 6. Writing it into the card

- Paraphrase. Describe speech patterns and write your own short example lines in the `Speech` section instead of copying canon dialogue. Long verbatim quotes are copyright-sensitive and also make the voice feel pasted in. A short, famous catchphrase may stay in the original language.
- Keep the real temperament. If the character is cruel, cold, or abrasive in the source, say so in `Personality`; softening it is the most common way a portrayal goes wrong.
- Fill `Sources consulted` in `Identity` with the URLs you actually read, or `model knowledge` if you used none. Do not list pages you did not open. This lets the user check where the portrayal came from and lets a later session decide whether to research more.
- Fill `Never Does` and `Deflection Style` from how the character behaves in the source, so boundaries can later be handled in their own manner.
